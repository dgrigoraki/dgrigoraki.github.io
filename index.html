01 — My Story

Where I'm from, and why I build.

I'm from Athens. I grew up in Ilion, Attica, in a family that taught me, without ever saying it directly, what it means to build something from nothing.

My parents run our family business, a carpet cleaning company that started decades ago as a tiny shop run by my grandparents. With the help of their children, it grew into one of the leading carpet cleaning businesses in Attica.

Watching my parents work hard for as long as I can remember, to give my siblings and me everything we needed, taught me something I've carried with me since. You have to love what you do, because that's what gets you through the hard times.

I always knew I wanted to do something different. Owning a company, working with technology, it felt like an enormous idea. I wanted it anyway.

For now, my plan is to work somewhere first, to learn how a business actually runs, to learn from the people around me, to build a network, and to gain as much knowledge as I can.

I want to build things that matter, not just things that exist.
I want to build something people will love, but mostly, something that helps them grow.

03 — Mindset

About economics & tech.

For me, data has never really been about the numbers. Every spreadsheet I've cleaned, every messy dataset I've untangled, is actually a question about people — what they buy, why they leave, what they actually need. Economics taught me how to ask that question. Data and AI are how I try to answer it.

Growing up around a family business, I saw up close how much invisible work goes into keeping something real running: the small decisions, the customers you can't afford to lose, the years it takes to earn trust. That's part of why I don't build for the sake of building. I want what I make, whether it's an agent, a dashboard, or a website, to actually solve a problem someone has, not just look impressive.

I think the most useful technology won't come from people who only understand code, or only understand markets. It will come from people who can sit in both worlds at once, and move between them without losing either. That's the builder I'm trying to become, one project at a time.

04 — Community

Learning by showing up.

I try to make time for seminars and conferences on economics, business, technology and the job market, alongside my studies and my own projects. Every event like that adds another piece to how I see this space and, more importantly, puts me in a room with people who are already doing what I want to do next.

I'm still early in building an actual network, but I'm working on it deliberately, one event, one conversation, one connection at a time. I want to keep learning, keep meeting people building things that matter, and eventually give back the way my family's hard work was once given to me.
